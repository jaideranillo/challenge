# Per-subscription cap must apply before the batch LIMIT, not after (LATERAL pattern)

`claimDue`'s original shape (FEAT-005) ranked candidates globally (`ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY ...)` + CTE), then filtered by the per-subscription in-flight cap, then applied the batch-wide `LIMIT`. This lets one saturated subscription's backlog fill the entire candidate pool with rows the cap then rejects, starving every other subscription's due rows out of the cycle — a single subscription with 10,000 due rows and a cap of 10 can silently block a subscription with just 1 due row from ever being claimed.

Fix (FEAT-006, TASK-006-01): restructure as a per-subscription `LATERAL` join. Driving relation is `subscriptions`; two `CROSS JOIN LATERAL` items follow — one computing `remaining_allowance = GREATEST(0, effective_cap - already_in_flight)`, one selecting that subscription's own capped, locked candidate set (`FOR UPDATE OF d SKIP LOCKED`, inner `ORDER BY next_attempt_at, delivery_id LIMIT remaining_allowance`). The outer query then applies `LIMIT :batch_limit` over the already-capped set. Must be `CROSS JOIN LATERAL` / `JOIN LATERAL ... ON TRUE`, never `LEFT JOIN LATERAL` — Postgres rejects a locking clause on the nullable side of an outer join, and a subscription with no admissible candidate must drop out entirely rather than contribute a null row. No window function needed or used — the inner `ORDER BY ... LIMIT` replaces the ranking a window function would have done, and unlike a window function it's legal alongside `FOR UPDATE`.

General lesson: whenever a query enforces a per-group cap over a candidate pool that will also be globally `LIMIT`ed, the cap must be applied per-group (LATERAL or equivalent) before the global limit — never as a post-filter over a globally-ranked/limited set.
